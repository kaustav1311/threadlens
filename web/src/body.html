How it works

  1. Export

    WhatsApp → the chat → More → Export chat → Without media.

  2. Drop it in

    Android or iPhone, 12- or 24-hour, .txt, .zip or a Word copy.

  3. Pick a lens

    Overview, Debate, Rigor, Personal or Work. Copy the report if you want it.

What does it actually measure?

Counting, mostly, done evenly. Questions vs assertions — people testing a claim ask, people defending one assert. Absolutist words (always, never, everyone), a validated marker of black-and-white thinking (Al-Mosaiwi & Johnstone, 2018). Evidence words. Heat, a 0–1 hostility heuristic from insults, slurs, put-downs and shouting, damped by message length. Tone from the open VADER lexicon (Hutto & Gilbert, 2014). Moral vocabulary after Moral Foundations Theory (Graham, Haidt & Nosek, 2009). Rhythm: who starts conversations, median reply times, after-hours messaging. Every number opens to show its working.

What is the Rigor lens?

A score out of 100 for how well each person argued — sourcing, answering the other's questions, staying on topic, conduct, hedging, and owning a mistake. It says nothing about who is right, and it cannot: no party, leader or policy appears anywhere in the word lists, and the repo ships two mirror-image sample arguments whose scores must swap when the sides swap, or the build fails.

Can it tell me if my relationship is in trouble?

No, and be suspicious of anything that says it can. It can show you that one of you starts 80% of the conversations, or that the warmth dropped off in March, or that neither of you has asked the other a question in six weeks. Those are worth talking about. They are not a diagnosis, and a chat is a thin slice of a relationship.

What will it get wrong?

Sarcasm, always. Quoting someone else's insult counts against you. Romanised Hindi and other mixed languages are only partly covered, so tone and heat undercount them. Comparisons switch off below 150 words per person, because short chats say nothing. Read the messages, not just the bars.

Is there a command-line version?

Yes. The repo has a Python CLI and an optional self-hosted API, both offline, plus local ML models (Detoxify, fallacy classifiers, self-contradiction detection) behind pip install 'threadlens[deep]'. threadlens analyse chat.zip --lens rigor --ledger claims.json exports the claim ledger for you (or an LLM) to fact-check.

Where your chat goes

Nowhere. That is the whole design.

How can I be sure?

This page ships a Content-Security-Policy of connect-src 'none', so the browser physically refuses to let it send your chat anywhere, even if the code tried. Open your network tab and watch nothing happen. Or save the page and run it with the Wi-Fi off — it works exactly the same. There is no server, no account, no cookie and no analytics.

Should I be analysing someone else's messages?

A chat you are in is also theirs. Use “Hide names” before you screenshot anything, keep reports to yourself, and do not publish another person's messages because a chart made them look bad.

Any limits?

25 MB, 250,000 messages, and 8 analyses per 10 minutes so a huge export cannot lock up your own browser. No server means nothing else needs limiting.
